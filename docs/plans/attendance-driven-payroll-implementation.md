Multiplying by 60 had understated daily-rated partial-work earnings by a factor of 60; daily/hourly API regressions now check an 11-day, ₱11,000 sample.
- The monthly-basis calculator was corrected so scheduled-period base is accrued for every scheduled date and unpaid absence is deducted once. It now aggregates and rounds the period once per consistent policy rather than rounding each date first. This prevents double absence deductions and daily rounding drift. - The payroll page can create one idempotent, period-scoped attendance draft (up to 200 expected employees atomically), with source revisions, formulas, and named blockers. In the final period of a calendar month, ordinary monthly, daily and hourly employees with fully resolved attendance and configured supported compensation receive separate SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG employee/employer lines and a source-versioned contribution snapshot. SSS and Pag-IBIG use actual regular and approved overtime earnings; PhilHealth uses fixed-basic monthly equivalent, excludes overtime and attendance deductions, and uses the confirmed divisor for time-based salary. Missing/non-contiguous tax history, unverified declarations, unsupported compensation classifications, termination contribution timing and required Form 2316 delivery remain blocked.
- Payroll review now opens with blocked roster entries marked `blocked`; an entry with blockers cannot be approved, but HR can explicitly exclude it with a required reason. A draft can be rebuilt while in review only until the first review/exclusion decision. Finalization checks changed or newly added attendance, salary, shift/pay-group assignments, leave, holidays, policies and overlapping approved/paid runs. Excluded employees are preserved in the run snapshot and reason, but their unresolved pay blockers do not prevent the remaining reviewed employees from finalizing.

The December BIR calculation records the opening balance and each finalized prior entry, checks gapless period coverage, computes annual tax and applies the difference as tax withholding or credit. Monthly contribution finalization also takes deterministic PostgreSQL transaction locks per employee/scheme/month before checking the ledger; the unique indexes remain a final integrity guard.
- Payroll policy confirmation now checks active effective-dated statutory configuration: SSS/Pag-IBIG ranges must be contiguous, PhilHealth must have one valid floor/ceiling row, and daily/weekly/semi-monthly/monthly BIR tables must cover every income band without a gap or overlap and include an open-ended top band. A missing table or one-cent gap prevents confirmation. This validates table shape only; it does not certify the entered amounts or supply official schedules.
- SSS configuration now represents compensation lower/upper bands and the mapped monthly salary credit separately from legacy MSC range columns. The calculator selects a row from monthly compensation; legacy rows without the new mapping do not match and cannot be used for policy confirmation. This avoids applying one flat contribution amount to a broad MSC range. Existing stored rows are preserved without guessed backfills and require an authorized re-entry using the official table before they can be used.
- PostgreSQL outbox models and an opt-in worker create PDFs from frozen snapshots only after an authorized finalization; delivery is disabled by default. The worker treats ambiguous SMTP outcomes as uncertain rather than retrying automatically.
- Payroll reviewers can download a finalized entry's PDF from the frozen outbox snapshot through an authenticated `payroll:view` endpoint. The response is private/no-store, draft downloads are rejected, and the request audit records the `payroll/payslip_download` action without logging pay data.
- Payroll editors can rebuild a blocked or stale unreviewed draft with a required reason. The operation supersedes the old draft, prepares a replacement from current inputs, and records both run IDs and the reason in the audit log. It refuses reviewed/excluded drafts and stale UI fingerprints.
- Monthly SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG snapshots retain every applicable schedule row's ID, effective date, update time and calculation values. Freshness checks compare the full active row set, so an in-place edit, removal or later-effective schedule invalidates the draft. Regression tests exercise all three schemes.

- Employee profiles now use the authenticated latest-finalized-payslip endpoint to show the most recent payroll period, gross/net amounts, employee-side deductions, and employer contributions in separate labeled sections. The payslip selector now orders by pay-period end date before creation time, so a later-created correction for an older period does not become the displayed latest payroll.

Verified minimum-wage-earner declarations now use the BIR exemption for statutory minimum wage and approved overtime, with zero period withholding outside the year-end adjustment and a source note retained in the frozen calculation trace. This treatment follows [BIR RR 8-2018](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No.%208-2018.pdf); [RR 10-2008](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/42126rr%20no.%2010-2008.pdf) ties private-sector eligibility to the statutory rate for the employee's assigned location and explains when additional taxable compensation disqualifies the MWE treatment. The profile requires the payroll approver to cite the applicable assigned-workplace wage order before verifying the classification. Taxable allowances remain blocked, so they cannot silently invalidate MWE eligibility. The system does not independently determine regional MWE eligibility or yet calculate night-shift, holiday, or hazard premiums; those inputs must remain blocked until wage-order and earning-category support is complete.

## Scope clarification and statutory follow-up (2026-10-07)

The first supported employee profile is ordinary local Philippine private-sector employment. Government, foreign/non-resident, self-employed/voluntary, household-worker and special-tax profiles are outside this first scope. Minimum-wage-earner status and unusual taxable/non-taxable compensation still require explicit employee data and rules; they must not be inferred from salary.

The confirmed collection policy is one employee and employer contribution collection per statutory type per calendar month, assigned to the last payroll run in that month. Policy confirmation now requires `frequency: once_monthly` and `collection_period: last_period`; the database ledger, reasoned correction linkage and final-period-only finalization guard are implemented. With resolved full-month attendance, ordinary monthly/daily/hourly drafts calculate SSS and Pag-IBIG from actual regular/approved-overtime earnings and PhilHealth from fixed basic salary. Daily/hourly PhilHealth monthly equivalent uses the confirmed monthly divisor, effective shift duration and effective salary periods; it deliberately ignores absences and overtime. Integration coverage checks daily/hourly payroll inputs for the final semi-monthly run. Opening tax-year declarations plus gapless finalized-period snapshots support BIR annualization. Minimum-wage-earner and cumulative-average tax cases remain blocked.

Review of [SSS's official employer/employee contribution schedule](https://www.sss.gov.ph/sss-contribution-table/) and [Circular 2024-006](https://www.sss.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CI-2024-006-Publication.pdf) confirms that employer bands map monthly compensation to an MSC; employee and employer MPF shares are separate above ₱20,000 MSC, and EC is employer-paid. The earlier model selected flat amounts by MSC ranges, so it could not represent the official compensation mapping accurately. The new schema separates compensation bands from the mapped MSC, calculates from compensation, and fails closed on legacy rows without the new mapping. Migration regression tests check the 61 seeded bands, representative boundaries, MSC mapping and shares against the circular. This verifies seeded 2025 values, but not later schedule amendments or employer approval.

Review of [HDMF Circular 460 as summarized by the Philippine Information Agency from a Pag-IBIG official](https://pia.gov.ph/news/employers-who-fail-to-remit-p200-pag-ibig-share-to-be-penalized/) confirmed the employee rates are 1% at or below ₱1,500 and 2% above it, the employer rate is 2%, and the contribution salary cap increased to ₱10,000 effective February 2024. The old Pag-IBIG selector also used only the first row of an effective schedule; existing calculator fixtures had the employee rates reversed and outdated expectations. The selector, fixtures and amount assertions are now corrected, with boundary and cap regressions. These tests validate the configured band mechanics; production schedule rows still require authorized source review before activation.

The [PhilHealth 2025-0002 advisory](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/advisories/2025/PA2025-0002.pdf) confirms the 2025 direct-contributor rate, floor and ceiling and says to use fixed monthly basic salary without reducing it for absences or unpaid leave. The [2026 circular archive](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/circulars/2026/archives.php) was reviewed; it does not show a new employer premium schedule, so 2026 rates still require official confirmation. The [BIR Annex E table](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/Annex%20E%20RR%2011-2018.pdf) is effective January 2023 onward, but withholding also depends on compensation classification and annual adjustment in [RR 11-2018](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No.%2011-2018.pdf). The [DBM 2024-2 circular letter](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/circular-letters?catid=335&id=2569%3Acircular-letter-no-2024-2&view=article) confirms the Pag-IBIG fund-salary cap increase; the underlying signed HDMF Circular 460 and schedule provenance still need to be retained with any activated configuration.

# Attendance-Driven Payroll Implementation

Status: in progress; not release-ready. Verification was run on 2026-10-08 against isolated local test data. No production data, production deployment, or real email delivery was used.

## Implemented in the current working branch

- Effective-dated employee shift assignments and Manila work dates, with UTC attendance timestamps and overnight shift attribution.
- Batch attendance import with server preflight/atomic commit, retained source/correction rows, duplicate employee/work-date and existing-record conflict detection, and recoverable import identities.
- Daily attendance interval revisions with overlap/date validation, actor/history records, a correction editor, and month/week/day/table views.
- Database guard for one active DTR per employee/work date; the migration aborts with duplicate record IDs and never rewrites legacy attendance.
- Overtime decisions record eligible and approved minutes, reason, reviewer and time; recalculation clears the prior decision and decision history is retained.
- Effective-dated employee pay-group assignments, configurable daily/twice-monthly/monthly schedules, versioned payroll-policy drafts and explicit confirmation.
- Missing-effective-salary roster filtering, employee compensation summary, and explicit selected-row bulk salary preflight/atomic commit.
- Payroll readiness endpoint and UI list expected employees with named salary, shift, pay-group, policy, attendance, pending-adjustment, overtime, duplicate and unscheduled-work blockers; responses are bounded and paginated.
- Legacy amount preview and draft generation are disabled because the old calculator has unsafe defaults and silent omissions. Historic runs remain readable, but statutory settings are drafts until confirmed. Automatic delivery and finalization have not been enabled.
- Duplicate same-employee/work-date CSV rows can be explicitly combined as intervals; missing-shift assignment is separately confirmed and audited. A merged day is not silently treated as several daily records.
- A Decimal/integer-minute attendance earnings calculator now handles hourly, daily, and monthly basis, approved overtime, explicit absence/paid-leave dispositions, monthly divisor, partial-day rule, and configured rounding. BIR bracket lookup uses one effective bracket and its cumulative base tax.
- Draft calculation enables daily and hourly salary bases, including BIR periodic withholding, and splits regular earnings across effective salary-date changes. For once-monthly contribution collection, SSS and Pag-IBIG use full-month regular earnings plus approved overtime; PhilHealth uses a separate contractual fixed-basic monthly equivalent. Daily/hourly PhilHealth conversion uses the confirmed monthly divisor and effective shift minutes, never actual worked days, so approved absences do not reduce its basis. Mid-month salary changes are split by effective dates.
- Fixed the legacy shift-duration unit bug in the payroll adapter: `total_hours_minus_lunch` is stored in minutes despite its name, so it must be passed to the earnings calculator as-is. Multiplying by 60 had understated daily-rated partial-work earnings by a factor of 60; daily/hourly API regressions now check an 11-day, ₱11,000 sample.
- The monthly-basis calculator was corrected so scheduled-period base is accrued for every scheduled date and unpaid absence is deducted once. It now aggregates and rounds the period once per consistent policy rather than rounding each date first. This prevents double absence deductions and daily rounding drift. An independent October 1–15, 2026 sample now gives ₱13,000.00 period base less ₱1,181.82 for one unpaid absence = ₱11,818.18 before statutory deductions; the API-level draft test verifies the same persisted figures and statutory blocker.
- The payroll page can create one idempotent, period-scoped attendance draft (up to 200 expected employees atomically), with source revisions, formulas, and named blockers. In the final period of a calendar month, ordinary monthly, daily and hourly employees with fully resolved attendance and configured supported compensation receive separate SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG employee/employer lines and a source-versioned contribution snapshot. SSS and Pag-IBIG use actual regular and approved overtime earnings; PhilHealth uses fixed-basic monthly equivalent, excludes overtime and attendance deductions, and uses the confirmed divisor for time-based salary. Allowances remain blocked until their scheme and tax treatment are configured. Earlier periods do not collect monthly contributions. Ordinary verified non-year-end employees receive BIR periodic withholding; December uses annualized 2023-onward rates from RR 11-2018, an opening-balance cutoff date and finalized in-system payroll entries. Missing/non-contiguous tax history, unverified declarations and minimum-wage-earner cases remain blocked.
- Review/finalization endpoints validate reviewer separation and snapshots. Finalization checks changed or newly added attendance, salary, shift/pay-group assignments, leave, holidays, policies and overlapping approved/paid runs. These guards are not a substitute for the missing complete calculation and are not reachable for the current blocked drafts.
- A monthly contribution ledger now records one base collection per employee/scheme/calendar-month, with reasoned correction rows. The confirmed company policy must explicitly select one collection in the final period; finalization stages the ledger atomically and rejects early-period deductions, missing/mismatched monthly snapshots, non-official source hosts and duplicate collections. Supported monthly/daily/hourly bases produce the snapshot when their prerequisites pass. The December BIR calculation records the opening balance and each finalized prior entry, checks gapless period coverage, computes annual tax and applies the difference as tax withholding or credit. Minimum-wage-earner and cumulative-average cases remain blocked.
- Payroll run details now expose employee names/codes, workflow state, per-entry blockers and calculation lines, reviewer actions with fingerprint checks, reason-required exclusions, and a separate final-approver action. Runtime finalization/delivery flags are returned by the detail API; the UI disables finalization and explains the acceptance gate while it is off. Finalized runs show durable delivery state, scheduled/sent/error details, audited address correction, reason-required retries, and explicit duplicate-risk confirmation for uncertain sends. Backend permissions and reviewer/preparer separation remain authoritative; this UI cannot bypass a blocked draft.
- The legacy bracket calculators now fail with a configuration conflict when the requested effective SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG or pay-period BIR schedule is missing or does not cover the input. They no longer present absent schedule data as a valid zero deduction. Monthly contribution finalization also takes deterministic PostgreSQL transaction locks per employee/scheme/month before checking the ledger; the unique indexes remain a final integrity guard.
- Payroll policy confirmation now checks active effective-dated statutory configuration: SSS/Pag-IBIG ranges must be contiguous, PhilHealth must have one valid floor/ceiling row, and daily/weekly/semi-monthly/monthly BIR tables must cover every income band without a gap or overlap and include an open-ended top band. A missing table or one-cent gap prevents confirmation. This validates table shape only; it does not certify the entered amounts or supply official schedules.
- SSS configuration now represents compensation lower/upper bands and the mapped monthly salary credit separately from legacy MSC range columns. The calculator selects a row from monthly compensation; legacy rows without the new mapping do not match and cannot be used for policy confirmation. This avoids applying one flat contribution amount to a broad MSC range. Existing stored rows are preserved without guessed backfills and require an authorized re-entry using the official table before they can be used.
- PostgreSQL outbox models and an opt-in worker create PDFs from frozen snapshots only after an authorized finalization; delivery is disabled by default. The worker treats ambiguous SMTP outcomes as uncertain rather than retrying automatically.
- Payroll reviewers can download a finalized entry's PDF from the frozen outbox snapshot through an authenticated `payroll:view` endpoint. The response is private/no-store, draft downloads are rejected, and the request audit records the `payroll/payslip_download` action without logging pay data.
- Finalization now has an independent default-off `PAYROLL_FINALIZATION_ENABLED` gate; the endpoint returns a clear unavailable response until HR accepts the parallel comparison and an operator explicitly enables it. Payslip delivery remains separately default-off.

## Scope clarification and statutory follow-up (2026-10-07)

The first supported employee profile is ordinary local Philippine private-sector employment. Government, foreign/non-resident, self-employed/voluntary, household-worker and special-tax profiles are outside this first scope. Minimum-wage-earner status and unusual taxable/non-taxable compensation still require explicit employee data and rules; they must not be inferred from salary.

The confirmed collection policy is one employee and employer contribution collection per statutory type per calendar month, assigned to the last payroll run in that month. Policy confirmation now requires `frequency: once_monthly` and `collection_period: last_period`; the database ledger, reasoned correction linkage and final-period-only finalization guard are implemented. With resolved full-month attendance, ordinary monthly/daily/hourly drafts calculate SSS and Pag-IBIG from actual regular/approved-overtime earnings and PhilHealth from fixed basic salary. Daily/hourly PhilHealth monthly equivalent uses the confirmed monthly divisor, effective shift duration and effective salary periods; it deliberately ignores absences and overtime. Integration coverage checks daily/hourly payroll inputs for the final semi-monthly run. Opening tax-year declarations plus gapless finalized-period snapshots support BIR annualization. Minimum-wage-earner and cumulative-average tax cases remain blocked.

Review of [SSS's official employer/employee contribution schedule](https://www.sss.gov.ph/sss-contribution-table/) and [Circular 2024-006](https://www.sss.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CI-2024-006-Publication.pdf) confirms that employer bands map monthly compensation to an MSC; employee and employer MPF shares are separate above ₱20,000 MSC, and EC is employer-paid. The earlier model selected flat amounts by MSC ranges, so it could not represent the official compensation mapping accurately. The new schema separates compensation bands from the mapped MSC, calculates from compensation, and fails closed on legacy rows without the new mapping. It preserves those rows without inventing a backfill. The contribution table still must be loaded and verified from the official source before use; current tests verify row selection and amount propagation using fixtures, not a complete official schedule.

Review of [HDMF Circular 460 as summarized by the Philippine Information Agency from a Pag-IBIG official](https://pia.gov.ph/news/employers-who-fail-to-remit-p200-pag-ibig-share-to-be-penalized/) confirmed the employee rates are 1% at or below ₱1,500 and 2% above it, the employer rate is 2%, and the contribution salary cap increased to ₱10,000 effective February 2024. The old Pag-IBIG selector also used only the first row of an effective schedule; existing calculator fixtures had the employee rates reversed and outdated expectations. The selector, fixtures and amount assertions are now corrected, with boundary and cap regressions. These tests validate the configured band mechanics; production schedule rows still require authorized source review before activation.

The [PhilHealth 2025-0002 advisory](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/advisories/2025/PA2025-0002.pdf) confirms the 2025 direct-contributor rate, floor and ceiling and says to use fixed monthly basic salary without reducing it for absences or unpaid leave. The [2026 circular archive](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/circulars/2026/archives.php) was reviewed; it does not show a new employer premium schedule, so 2026 rates still require official confirmation. The [BIR Annex E table](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/Annex%20E%20RR%2011-2018.pdf) is effective January 2023 onward, but withholding also depends on compensation classification and annual adjustment in [RR 11-2018](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No.%2011-2018.pdf). The [DBM 2024-2 circular letter](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/circular-letters?catid=335&id=2569%3Acircular-letter-no-2024-2&view=article) confirms the Pag-IBIG fund-salary cap increase; the underlying signed HDMF Circular 460 and schedule provenance still need to be retained with any activated configuration.

Do not merge or deploy this implementation as a completed payroll phase. The persisted draft is explicitly provisional and finalization remains disabled. Statutory schedules and company conversion rules still require payroll-owner review, and the browser tests do not exercise a successful full review/finalization/delivery journey. Keep finalization and email delivery disabled until remaining calculations, HR-approved parallel sample comparison, complete browser flow and delivery fault-injection coverage pass.
